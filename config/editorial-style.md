# Editorial style guide: v3 — APPROVED by the owner on 2026-10-25

This guide comes from the three Phase 2 art studies in `prototypes/art/`. It becomes the quality bar once the owner approves it. Changes apply going forward only; published entries are never restyled.

## News focus (owner request, 2026-10-25)
- **Audience:** a reader in Boston, USA. Use real headlines from major outlets.
- **Priority:**
  1. The top **US national** stories: what major US outlets are leading with.
  2. **Boston / Massachusetts / New England** stories when significant locally.
  3. **International** stories only when major US outlets lead with them or they directly affect the US.
- The ranking code applies this automatically (`src/news/cluster.py`, `config/sources.json` → `editorial_focus`). The editor (Claude) still makes the final call and writes the reason in the brief.

## Visual identity (round 2: "graphic poster", 2026-10-25)
- **Look:** bold graphic-poster editorial illustration, like magazine covers and vintage travel posters. Flat shapes, a limited palette of about 4–6 colours per piece, and a printed-paper grain (`grain_filter()` in `src/art/svg.py`). It should look designed, not photographic or AI-generated.
- **People first:** the main story is shown through **people doing the thing the news is about** (speaking, voting, launching, evacuating, celebrating, protesting), drawn with the people kit (`src/art/people.py`). Scenery supports the people; it is never the whole picture.
- **Format:** SVG, 1200×900 (4:3). Generated by a Python script, with a fixed random seed so a script always redraws the same picture.
- **Palette:** chosen per story. Keep strong contrast between figures and background, and use one accent colour for the focal figure or object.
- **Depth:** a background shape (sky bands, wall, a giant sun or spiral), a middle ground (buildings, vehicles) and foreground figures.
- **Text in art:** none, apart from real signage that is essential and verified. Never invent slogans, signs or quotes.

## Recognizability rules
- Real places and objects are drawn from their known features. Examples: the Secretariat's slab, its dark mechanical-floor bands and its marble end walls; the Chrysler crown's stacked arches; a hurricane's counter-clockwise turn in the Northern Hemisphere.
- When a detail can't be verified (a specific rocket's markings, a person's face), stay generic rather than guess.
- **Literal first:** draw what actually happened in the headline (the crash, the storm, the person doing the thing), so it can be understood at a glance. Symbolism is a supporting layer, never a replacement.
- **Public figures** (leaders, celebrities, athletes) may be drawn as recognizable editorial caricatures using documented traits: hair, build, signature clothing, and the setting. Conditions:
  - only doing what reliable sources confirm they did, where they did it
  - no invented quotes, speech bubbles or text
  - no demeaning distortion beyond normal caricature; the tone stays neutral-factual, and the drama comes from the event, not from mocking the person
- **Private individuals** (victims, bystanders, ordinary people in the news) stay generic and unrecognizable.
- Faces: blank by default; key figures get a simple profile face (eye, brow, mouth) with an expression that matches what was reported.
- **Disasters and tragedies:** show the event and the response (wreckage, smoke, rescuers, shelters), never bodies, injuries or suffering faces. Never show casualties beyond what is reported, and never show any at all in detail.
- Crowds show a realistic mix of people. Never invent the size or mood of a crowd beyond what sources report.

## Quality checklist (each piece must pass before publishing)
1. **Focal point:** the main story reads in about 2 seconds at phone size (≈360px wide).
1a. **People and action:** if the story involves people, they are visible doing the reported action.
1b. **Literal read:** someone who saw only the headline would recognize the event in the picture.
2. **Recognizable:** at least one concrete, story-specific subject. Generic symbols alone fail.
3. **Faithful to the facts:** nothing drawn contradicts the verified facts. No invented casualties, crowds, signs, people or outcomes.
4. **Supporting stories:** at most two, and only if they fit one coherent scene.
5. **Composition:** clear hierarchy and intentional empty space; no collage of small icons.
6. **Technical:** valid XML; 1200×900 with `viewBox`; `<title>` and `<desc>` present; no `<script>`, event handlers or external links; file under 250 KB.
7. **Alt text:** describes what is drawn and which stories it represents, without overstating.
8. **Variety:** composition and palette differ clearly from the previous 3 entries.

## Effort limits (measured on the 3 studies)
- Each piece took **1 draft + 2 self-critique revisions**, and the second revision fixed the biggest flaws in every case. Proposed limit: **at most 3 render–inspect rounds per daily piece**. If it still fails the checklist, log it and publish nothing rather than publish weak art.
- Output size was 75–140 KB per SVG in round 1 and 21–90 KB in round 2.
- Usage: I couldn't measure exact tokens per piece in this session. Measure it during the Phase 4 dry runs before fixing daily limits.

## Known limits of the SVG method
- **Round 1** (painted-looking landscapes) was rejected by the owner: too much scenery, not enough people or story, not interesting enough.
- **Round 3** (literal headline scenes) works for disasters, vehicles, weather and public-figure caricature. Caricature likeness is suggestive (hair, build, clothes, setting) rather than portrait-accurate.
- **Round 2** (graphic poster with people) is good at people doing actions, crowds, vehicles, buildings, weather and symbolic shapes.
- **Still weak:** recognizable likenesses of specific people (deliberately avoided), animals in detail, and complex hand interactions such as handshakes or holding small objects precisely.
- Each new kind of scene may need new kit pieces (poses, props). Building these pieces up over time is part of the daily pipeline.
